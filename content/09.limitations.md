# Discussion

## What another researcher can adopt

The reusable contribution is the operating arrangement: a project-specific contract, a shared accepted-state record, versioned artifacts, an evidence-based review cycle, and explicit handoffs. The guide and templates make that arrangement concrete; the development Skill packages instructions for participating in it. Adoption does not require reproducing the investigator's scientific projects, but it does require assigning responsibility for the record and testing that the chosen participants can retrieve the required artifacts.

The case also identifies work that remains outside a bounded assignment. As a project changes, someone must recognize that a result invalidates an assumption, affects another workstream, or changes which records govern. In this implementation the User performed that integration. Storing context was insufficient when a session did not recognize which available records mattered. Selective context assembly is therefore an operational strategy for adopters to test, not a demonstrated general solution to model context limitations.

The materials should be adapted to project risk, duration, available tools, and the burden of maintaining them. They are not presented as best practice established by comparison. Changing the provider combination, adding participants, delegating repository writes, or introducing automated routing changes the implementation and requires its own evaluation. In particular, packaging the protocol as a Skill does not demonstrate that an assistant will follow it reliably.

## Evidence and generalizability

The unit of replication is the investigator, not the log record or workspace. Hundreds of records under one person's direction remain one case. There was no control condition, randomized assignment, blinding, or prospectively defined comparative outcome. The study cannot separate the effects of a durable record, human coordination, provider diversity, or the investigator's expertise and cannot establish superiority over alternative workflows.

Rejected proposals were under-recorded because unpromising ideas were often abandoned in conversation. A rejection rate would therefore reflect recording practice as well as the underlying process. Full chat histories were not part of the frozen corpus, and historical model identity and prior exposure were not consistently captured. Aligning timestamped chat exports with log records could recover some interaction sequences, but hidden context and unrecorded tool state might remain unavailable. Procedurally separate reviews would still not establish statistical independence between models.

The current protocol contains refinements added after the analytical cutoff, including separate review and approval fields and prospective identity and exposure records. Their presence in the guide does not repair historical missingness. The Skill package and adoption by other investigators were not tested in the frozen corpus. Lower-cost plans, open-source substitutes, and two sessions from one provider were not evaluated as equivalent alternatives.

## Coordination burden and tool dependence

The workflow required substantial User time, three paid subscriptions, and continued attention to routing, artifact custody, and record maintenance. Monetary cost and the value of subscription use relative to token-billed APIs were not measured. Product and connector behavior also varied over time; the reported configurations and failures describe the observed implementation, not a stable specification of current or future services. These dependencies limit direct reproducibility and make a small adoption pilot important before extending the workflow to a larger project.
