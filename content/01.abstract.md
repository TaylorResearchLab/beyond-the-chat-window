# Abstract

Sustained scientific work with AI assistants requires a way to preserve project state, review contributions, and connect decisions to the files and evidence that support them. This paper presents a human-governed workflow that researchers can adapt to work across AI platforms. It separates a structured shared record, versioned artifacts, and User-controlled execution, and specifies a task-review-acceptance cycle with explicit handoffs. A reusable work contract, implementation guide, templates, and development-stage Skill provide practical adoption materials. The workflow was developed through one investigator's coordination of GPT and Claude using Notion and GitHub. A frozen retrospective corpus contained 728 records from seven standardized Collaboration Logs across six active workspaces; manuscript production provided a separate, later case. Both platforms led tasks, reviewed contributions, and corrected the other's work. Observed failures included unlogged decisions, inaccessible artifacts, incomplete execution records, invalid checksums, and unsupported claims and citations. These failures motivated explicit recording, transfer, verification, and review controls. Shared records supported continuity, but relevant context could remain unused until the investigator directed attention to it. The retrospective study characterizes use and limitations; it does not establish comparative effectiveness or usability across investigators. Later protocol refinements and the Skill package were not evaluated throughout the frozen corpus. The practical contribution is an adaptable workflow and implementation resources, with documented responsibilities and limits rather than a claim of autonomous scientific coordination.
