# Abstract

This work presents a practical workflow to help scientists adopt collaborative AI while maintaining scientific direction and a rigorous research record that supports reproducibility. The human investigator, termed the User, formulates questions, assigns tasks, evaluates evidence, integrates findings, and redirects the research as it develops. AI participants contribute through a structured shared record linked to versioned code, documented execution, reciprocal review, and explicit handoffs across sessions. Recording requirements connect decisions and interpretations to the sources, methods, inputs, commands, outputs, and revisions needed to examine and reproduce the work. A reusable work contract, implementation guide, templates, and development-stage Skill provide starting points for adaptation to other projects. The design was informed by one investigator's coordination of GPT and Claude through Notion and GitHub. A frozen retrospective corpus comprised 728 records from seven standardized Collaboration Logs across six active workspaces, with manuscript production examined separately. Both platforms contributed as task leads and reviewers and corrected the other's work. Incomplete records, inaccessible artifacts, and unsupported claims and citations motivated explicit verification and recording practices. Maintaining continuity also required the User to identify relevant context and redirect work as scientific questions changed. This single-investigator descriptive study does not establish comparative effectiveness or general usability, and the Skill package was not evaluated in the frozen corpus. The contribution is an adaptable method for User-directed AI collaboration that makes scientific decisions, evidence, and revisions available for scrutiny and reuse.
