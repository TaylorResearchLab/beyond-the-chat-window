# Future evaluation

A prospective study should compare matched tasks under several conditions: one AI platform, multiple named sessions from one provider, the User-mediated two-provider workflow described here, and an API-orchestrated workflow. Assignments, prior exposure to another participant's work, review events, corrections, and acceptance decisions should be recorded when they occur rather than reconstructed later.

Outcomes should be defined before data collection and should include detection of substantive defects, reproducibility of accepted results, time to an accepted artifact, number and importance of revisions, total User review time, token use, and monetary cost. Review should be stored as an append-only event rather than inferred from a checkbox that can later be cleared.

A multi-investigator study would test a more difficult situation in which no single person holds continuous project memory, final authority, and access control. Such a design would require a written dispute process, explicit responsibility for maintaining the shared record, and a method for resolving conflicts among investigators as well as among model outputs.

Prospective measurement will itself add work. Future studies should therefore measure the burden created by their logging requirements and distinguish information that is essential for reproducibility from instrumentation that interferes with active research.
